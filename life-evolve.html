Evolving Life on a Torus

Conway's Life with two rival clans (warm, cool) — but each live cell now carries a heritable gene: its tolerance τ to the rival clan. Selection acts at the borders, and you watch the population evolve.

The gene: tolerance τ

Neighbours of the same clan help (+1 each), the rival clan hinders. How much the rival hinders depends on the cell's τ ∈ [0,1]: the effective support is allies − (1−τ)·foes. τ=0 is full rivalry (a rival counts −1, the classic behaviour); τ=1 ignores rivals entirely. A cell survives when that support lands in the S range. (Birth still counts rivals in full — τ is a survival advantage only.)

Inheritance & selection

A newborn inherits the average τ of its same-clan parents, plus a random mutation (σ slider; σ=0 freezes the genes as a control). At a clan border, low-τ cells die and high-τ cells survive to breed — so border colonies drift toward higher tolerance on their own. That is natural selection: variation (mutation) filtered by differential survival.

Neighborhood

Moore is the classic 8 cells. Weighted 5×5 counts 24 cells with distance-decaying weights (rules become ranges Bx-y/Sp-q). The bigger kernel keeps clans pressed against each other in dense fronts — so tolerance has somewhere to evolve. Foe weight there is large, making the selection pressure (and the brightening borders) much stronger than under Moore.

Environment (the terrain)

A static landscape underlies the grid: each cell is fertile (green) or hostile (red). Terrain adds a bias to the birth/survival support — Terrain strength sets how much (0 = off) — so life pools in fertile hollows and shuns hostile ground, which becomes barriers and dead zones. It shapes where life grows; τ still evolves within it.

Generate lays down a random terrain-like landscape (basins and ridges); Flat clears it. The Terrain tool paints it by hand — pick Fertile or Hostile and drag. The Colour button cycles Clan → Tolerance → Environment; the last shows the raw red/green field (empty ground is tinted by it in every mode, so you always see the landscape under the ecology).

Hatch a creature (†)

The † presets are Larger-than-Life creatures — coherent blobs (lace disc, ring, blob) that only survive as a single clan: a rival's foe-pressure destroys them, so a mixed Random never grows one. Selecting a † preset therefore clears the grid and drops one compact patch of the selected clan (pick Warm/Cool first); press Play and it grows into the creature. It seeds at τ₀, so raise Seed tolerance before hatching if you want a tolerant creature.

Then the evolution tie-in: with the creature running, switch to Pen, pick the other clan, and paint a patch against its flank. The contact border is where foe-pressure bites — watch that edge select for higher τ (brighter in Clan colour, or use the Tolerance heatmap). A creature under attack evolving a tolerant skin is the whole idea, in miniature.

Seeing it

Cell brightness = τ: dim cells are vulnerable (low τ), bright cells tolerant (high τ). The Colour button switches to a τ heatmap (indigo→green→yellow) that ignores clan.

Under the simple Moore rule, selection pushes the whole population up and the graph's mean-τ lines climb from the 0.5 seed. Under a weighted band-pass rule the story is subtler and more lifelike: contested borders evolve high tolerance (they'd starve without it) while crowded interiors stay low (there, tolerance tips a cell into overcrowding) — so the global mean can sit near 0.5 while the τ heatmap shows bright adapted fronts over dim cores. Adaptation is local. Turn mutation to 0 as a control: the genes freeze and nothing adapts.

Controls

Reference — prior art

This toy sits at the crossing of three well-studied families of cellular automata. A map of the ideas it borrows, for further reading.

Weighted / large neighbourhoods

  • Weighted Life — per-cell weight matrix + thresholds; the 5×5 kernel here is one. LifeWiki.
  • Larger than Life — Kellie Evans; radius-r neighbourhoods with interval birth/survival (the Bx-y/Sp-q notation). Wikipedia.
  • SmoothLife — Stephan Rafler, 2011; the continuous-space limit, swimming gliders. arXiv:1111.1567.
  • Lenia — Bert Chan, 2018; smooth kernels + continuous states, a whole zoo of lifeforms. lenia project.
  • MNCA — Slackermanz; multiple concentric neighbourhoods, membrane/mitosis visuals. write-up.

Multiple species / colours

  • Immigration — 2-colour Life, newborn takes the majority parent colour (~1971). LifeWiki.
  • QuadLife — 4 colours, ties resolve to the absent colour (bias-free). LifeWiki.
  • Cyclic cellular automaton — Griffeath; rock–paper–scissors predation, spiral waves. Wikipedia.
  • Wa-Tor — A. K. Dewdney, Scientific American 1984; predator–prey on a torus. Wikipedia.

Stochastic rules

  • Probabilistic cellular automata — noisy birth/survival; the extinction–survival transition sits in the directed-percolation universality class. Directed percolation. (See the sibling life-stochastic.html.)

Environment & evolution

  • Sugarscape — Epstein & Axtell, 1996; agents on a resource landscape — the model behind this project's planned environment field. Wikipedia.
  • Inhomogeneous CA — per-cell non-uniform rules/parameters (a terrain layer). Cellular automaton.
  • Evoloop — Hiroki Sayama, 1999; self-replicating loops that mutate under selection. Wikipedia.
  • Tierra / Avida — digital-evolution systems where programs mutate and compete. Avida, Tierra.
