Squirrels & Pine Martens — Ecology on a Torus

A spatial contact process on a torus: two rival prey species compete for empty ground. Red squirrels (native) and grey squirrels (invasive) each colonise an adjacent empty cell at their own per-species birth rate β; an occupied cell survives each tick with probability σ.

Why grey tends to win

Grey's default β is set higher than red's — the invasive-species advantage. With no predation yet, a higher β simply out-colonises empty ground faster, so grey expands and red's share shrinks over time. Watch the two lines in the population chart cross.

The pine marten

A conserved predator (brown, drawn dim when hungry and bright when fed). Each tick it loses δ energy to metabolism and gains g by eating one adjacent prey. Energy caps at E_cap (15); a marten with energy ≤0 starves and dies. A well-fed marten (energy ≥ E_breed) can spill a new marten into an empty neighbour cell at rate μ, paying a breeding cost. Use Reintroduce martens to drop a sparse scatter onto the current grid, or pick Marten in Pen species to place them by hand.

Evasion — why any of this works

Reds co-evolved with the marten and evade it; greys are North American, ground-naive, and mostly do not. That single asymmetry is the whole mechanism. Let the greys take over, then hit Reintroduce martens: greys crash, reds recover.

The control case is one click. Make evasion symmetric sets red's evasion equal to grey's — same predator, same predation pressure, only the asymmetry removed. The reds die anyway. A predator that eats both species does not save the native one; it just harvests both. Predation alone is not the cause — the asymmetry is.

Watch the marten line too. Nothing couples it to the greys explicitly, but because hunting an evading red usually wastes the turn, martens can only live off greys — so their numbers track grey numbers, greys rebound as martens fade, and the pair settles into a cycle. That is emergent, not coded.

Other food — and why it changes everything

Real martens are generalists: voles, birds, eggs, berries, carrion. Their numbers do not depend on squirrels. Other food adds that. It has to be a shared resource — a flat food bonus would just be a rename of a lower δ, since e−δ+food is only e−(δ−food). So each marten's share is divided by how many martens crowd around it, which is what gives martens a baseline density set by the countryside rather than by squirrels. Set it to 0 to switch the subsidy off.

Turning it on stops the boom-bust cycle: a subsidised marten does not starve when the greys thin out, so the greys never get their rebound and are wiped out completely rather than cycling. That matches Ireland, where greys vanished from marten country. But push Other food past ~2 and the martens take the reds down too — a predator propped up by other food has no reason to stop. Ecologists call this hyperpredation; it is how cats fed by introduced rabbits have exterminated island birds.

Now the strange one. Set Other food to 0 and raise Red evades to 0.95: the reds do worse, and the greys take over. With no other food, the marten lives only on squirrels — so reds that evade too well starve the very predator that is keeping the greys off them. Unsubsidised, the reds have to feed their own bodyguard. Only with both the other food and good evasion (the defaults) do you get the real outcome: greys gone, reds abundant, martens steady.
